Financial Literacy for High School Students

Helping teens build smart money habits

Created by Gavin Siglar

Why Financial Literacy Matters

Financial literacy gives students the knowledge and confidence to manage money responsibly. Without these skills, many young adults face debt, poor credit, and financial stress. Learning early helps students make smarter long-term decisions.

Core Financial Concepts

Budgeting

Budgeting helps students track income and expenses so they don’t overspend.

Saving & Emergency Funds

Emergency savings protect against unexpected costs like car repairs or medical bills.

Credit & Debt

Understanding interest rates and credit scores helps avoid long-term financial trouble.

Investing Basics

Investing allows money to grow over time through compound interest.

Interactive Financial Scenarios

Scenario 1: You Just Got Paid

You earned $300 from a part-time job.

Saving half helps build financial security and prepares you for emergencies or future goals.

Spending everything may feel good short-term but leaves no money for unexpected expenses.

Scenario 2: Emergency Expense

Your phone breaks and costs $180 to repair.

Using emergency savings prevents debt and avoids paying interest.

Using a credit card may lead to interest charges if not paid off quickly.

Scenario 3: First Credit Card

You receive an offer for a credit card with a 22% interest rate.

Paying the full balance avoids interest and builds a strong credit score.

Paying only the minimum increases debt and costs more over time.

Scenario 4: Saving for a Car

You want to buy a $6,000 car in one year.

Saving monthly avoids interest and builds strong financial habits.

Loans add interest, increasing the total cost of the car.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Why should high school students learn financial literacy?

It prepares students to manage money, avoid debt, and make informed financial decisions early.

What happens if you don’t learn financial literacy?

Lack of financial knowledge often leads to debt, poor credit, and financial stress.

Is budgeting really necessary?

Yes. Budgeting helps control spending and ensures money is available for needs and goals.

How does this website help students?

It provides real-world scenarios, explanations, and tools to practice smart financial decisions.
